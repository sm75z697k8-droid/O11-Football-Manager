# Phase 6.9B — Physical pass-to-receiver reliability

## User report and evidence

The user reported that attempted passes repeatedly appeared disconnected from teammates despite the previous AI improvements. Instead of adding another positional bonus, this release measures actual **receiver contact** across the deterministic seeded matches.

Before changes, a nine-seed exhibition diagnostic recorded **104 kicked AI passes**, of which **79 reached the intended player's physical control**. Sixteen reached another player, seven ran out of play, and two remained in flight / timed out in the sample. Some misses passed 1–3 metres beyond an accessible teammate; others were aimed toward players close to the touchline. This is not a professional pass-completion-rate calibration: it is a reproducible *intended-receiver control* measurement.

### Root causes

1. **Disconnected kick selection:** The on-ball planner previously treated a visually open teammate as a candidate without actually checking whether the player could reach the ball using the existing 3.2s physical interception estimator.
2. **Over-extrapolated / stale meeting targets:** Passes led the receiver by 0.3s of current velocity even though the moment an intent exists the receiving AI stops following its old support instruction.
3. **Uncalibrated angular spread:** For an ordinary AI pass, the original angular-error envelope could send an otherwise sensible 10–20m ball more than one metre from the intended receiver; facing away worsened this. This is reduced **only for AI passes** and remains attribute-/body-/seed-sensitive.
4. **Outdated chase fallback:** If a physically inaccurate kick escaped the 3.2s interception estimate, the receiver kept running toward the old intended point instead of looking toward the actual moving ball.

### Implementation

- A new pure `src/game/ai/pass-viability.ts` estimates a pass's physical launch using the **same attribute- and power-dependent velocity equation** as authoritative contact physics. It creates a temporary predicted ball and asks `estimateInterception` whether the real nominated teammate could intercept it. A pass with no physically plausible reception isn't included in the on-ball candidate list. **No actual kick or ball movement is generated by this forecast.**
- The intended meeting point is near the **teammate's current feet**, with at most 1.6m of lead based on physical braking distance, rather than extrapolating an old support run. Teammate movement and closed passing lanes remain part of the existing short-horizon utility score.
- A modest width/accuracy penalty discourages risky passes aimed right against the touchline. It does not remove wide attacking options or force a central pass.
- Ordinary AI pass angular variation is reduced to a **still imperfect, seed-sensitive estimate**, with Passing, Technique and Composure affecting errors. Passing while badly oriented retains extra execution risk. No pass is made automatically successful. Diagnostic kicks, manually scheduled kicks and AI shooting retain their original angular formula.
- `src/game/ai/pass-coordination.ts` updates the intended recipient toward the actual moving ball when a physically accurate interception forecast is unavailable, rather than sending them to an obsolete pass target. Player locomotion still applies its own speed, turning and acceleration limits; defending players may physically intercept or force deflections.
- State format/checkpoint stays v9; engine algorithm version is updated to protect deterministic compatibility. Existing kick-off, on-ball AI, seeded replay, contact outcomes, defensive coordination and UI continue operating normally.

### Quality gates and remaining limitations

A repeatable nine-seed real-match diagnostic tracks attempted AI passes and the next physical receiver touch. Unit/integration tests additionally cover reachable and unreachable options, body-dependent accuracy, touchline margin, recovery after a misplaced ball, player-speed-compatible targets, no fake contacts and deterministic checkpoint replay.

This is **not** a completed tactical pass engine, pass success roll or a guaranteed pass-completion threshold. The predicted receiver intercept is a simplified optimistic acceleration model and may still fail due to physical execution, active defender challenges and first-touch ability. Professional pass accuracy and physical biomechanics have not been empirically calibrated. Full restarts, team-level run coordination, referee and long matches remain unfinished. Tests run in GitHub Actions (Node), not a real iPhone Safari performance profile.
